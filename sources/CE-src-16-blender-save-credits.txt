Use Case 2 — The Dialogue Scene
The Blocking

What it does: Builds the final dialogue previs in one brief: six seat-locked color-coded proxies around a table (the color is the identity), a four-cut camera plan on spline rails, and slow realistic handheld.












Create a new Blender project. Blocking: a round single-leg table and 6 people sitting around it on chairs. 24fps, 21:9, 30 seconds. The people are simple seated proxies: just sitting, hands on the table. Don't animate the heads. Give them contrasting colors — that's their identity: red, green, blue, yellow, purple, cyan. So the viewport instantly reads who is who. Remember them, they'll be separate characters. Add a floor and a round wall so the interior reads, everything neutral grey, one light.

Now the camera. All movement on spline rails, the camera targets a null, everything floats. The orbit on a wider lens, the dialogue cuts closer, and the zoom doesn't jump a single frame on the cuts. 4 cuts for 30 seconds: 1 — about half the video: a slow orbit around the table, camera slightly above the table. We only pass behind red at the start and cyan in the middle. The target hands off across the table, face to face: yellow → blue → green, slowing on each but never stopping. 2 — about three seconds: cyan over blue's shoulder. Camera below shoulder height, barely crawling sideways. 3 — 2–3 seconds: red over yellow's shoulder, camera crawling the same way. 4 — the rest, long: purple over green's shoulder, halfway through the gaze slides along the eyeline to cyan without a cut, and it ends on him. Green stays the near mass the whole cut, and nobody blocks cyan at the end.

Add handheld — but real, slow handheld, not wiggle: a lazy body sway in long waves on the camera plus a very small tremor. On the target — slow drift and breathing: a bit livelier during the orbit, completely lazy in the OTS cuts. No fast jitter. And check the cuts between shots: camera, target and zoom change strictly on the cut, not a single transition frame. Save a backup copy of the file after every stage.

The Blocking

What it does: Builds the final hypermotion ad blocking in one brief: 13 scenes with per-scene cameras, commercial speed-ramp motion, and black gaps left for the liquid-sim generations. The can is a cylinder, the ice is cubes, the fruit is spheres — pure camera work and timing.




Build me a scene in Blender: 30 seconds, 24fps, a hypermotion CGI ad for a canned soda. The can, the fruit and the ice are just spheres and cubes as blocking. Every scene gets its own camera through markers, the moves sharp and commercial. Between some of the scenes leave black gaps a couple of seconds long — that's where the liquid-sim generations will drop in later. The story scene by scene:
1 — the hook: a ball drops from above like a bullet, hits the water, the can shoots up out of the impact point with a couple of fruits around it, everything freezes — the can splits into three horizontal slices, the fruits crack in half, then it all snaps back together and the can does one sharp full turn at a tilt.
2 — the camera spirals up around the can from the water, berries floating up around it.
3 — the camera holds at the lid, then travels down along the can.
4 — wide shot: the camera arcs around the can, five colored clone cans fan out from behind it one by one and get sucked back in.
5 — close-up: the can spins slowly in the right half of the frame, ice cubes float up on the left, in front and behind it.
6 — view from inside a translucent glass, looking up: the can hangs almost horizontal above it, like it's pouring.
7 — GAP.
8 — the camera circles the can and the glass from above; put a grid on the floor.
9 — balls fall from below upward and freeze like they've hit water.
10 — the clones come down from the sky and hang in a ring around the main can; the camera flies low between the cans, touching nothing.
11 — macro cut: lemons, limes and ice fly up through the frame in an endless stream at different depths, almost all in blur; the frame must never be empty for a second.
12 — the reveal: the can lifts off and spins, five clones bloom around it one by one. The camera slowly circles.
13 — packshot: the can levitates on the left, a couple of berries next to it, and on the right a white "Higgs" wordmark bounces up letter by letter with a spring, font bold and rounded.
In every scene the camera should snap in hard, sag in the middle and accelerate into the cut — like a speed ramp. Check that nothing intersects anywhere: the clones with the can, the ice with the can, the letters with each other. Make the end text bigger, but so it fits in the frame whole, and balance the composition — can on the left, wordmark on the right.
