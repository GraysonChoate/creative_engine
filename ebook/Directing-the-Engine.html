Directing the Engine

How to tell Claude and Higgsfield what you see in your head. Pick a tab, copy a phrase, use it.

Start

Three steps for any shot. Each tab below does one of them.

  1. 1
    Pick the world. Real, painted, or invented. Write its rules. (World tab)
  2. 2
    Pick the camera move and the effect. Copy the phrase. (Camera, Effects)
  3. 3
    Write the prompt in the model's words. Fix it with one-line changes. (Say It, Fix)

Five rules that matter

The world makes sense.
Where, when, and why. Nothing random.
Effects need a real cause.
The bottle opens, then the liquid swirls.
The camera is free.
Sky to ant. Hands to shoe threads.
Real labels stay real.
Text comes from the real source. Check every result.
Fix small.
One-line change first. Rewrite last.
Quote credits first.
Always, before any spend.

World

Decide what kind of world this is before any image. Then keep to its rules.

Pick one

WorldUse forRule
RealLiv Body, XGT, any live-action brand adPeople, places, actions match real life. Camera and light can do impossible things.
Stylized realAnime, painted looks (Nikomachia)Real-world logic in a set look. Buildings, clothes, geography fit the culture and era. Fighting can be elevated but follows the world's own physics.
InventedWild cartoon, a brand-new worldMake up the rules, write them down, never break them.

Three layers

LayerRuleExample
AnchorsAlways true to the world: people, product, place, reason.Runner, bottle, her kitchen, morning.
EffectsFantasy allowed. Each starts from a real cause, then returns to the real.She opens the bottle, liquid rises around her, she drinks.
CameraFree. Goes anywhere.Drop from the sky into the kitchen. Zoom into shoe threads.

Test: "What real thing triggers this?" If nothing does, cut it or give it a trigger. If the place cannot hold the action, change the action, not the physics.

Right and wrong

WorksMix electrolytes in the kitchen on waking. Run. Come back. Robots fighting on real Manhattan streets.
BreaksMixing electrolytes on a rock in the middle of a lake. Upside-down buildings in Manhattan.

Fill this in first

WORLD: [real / stylized real / invented]
RULES: 1) … 2) … 3) …
WHERE / WHEN / WHY: …
ANCHORS: people, product, place, reason
EFFECTS (each with its real cause): …
CAMERA: …

In short

  • Pick the world type, write 3 to 5 rules, add where, when, why.
  • Anchors fit the world. Effects need a real cause. Camera is free.
  • Check every storyboard frame against the rules.

Camera

Find the move. Copy the phrase. Give it a real subject.

Basic words the model knows

One camera move per shot. Use numbers, not adjectives: degrees, seconds, counts.

In short

  • One move per shot. Name it with the exact phrase.
  • Say lens numbers (29° to 107°), not "wide-ish".
  • Slow and purposeful unless the tone is action.

Effects

Impossible things that still start from something real in the shot.

Look and light

Write a style header once (under 80 words): lens and format, light, color grade, film grain, acting style, sound rule. Paste it at the start of every prompt.

Put film grain and the anamorphic look in locations and video prompts. Keep character and prop sheets plain: gray background, flat light, no grain.

In short

  • Name the real source of every effect.
  • Loops: last frame equals first frame.
  • Real logo and real label. Never generated.

Say It

Your words on the left. The words the model understands on the right.

Prompt order, same every shot

[STYLE HEADER]
[REFERENCE ROLES] image 1 = hero identity, image 2 = location, image 3 = pack label
[SPACE] left / right, door counts, where the camera starts
[BEATS] 3 sentences or fewer each
[CAMERA] one move
[COUNT LOCKS] exactly 3 riders
[KEEP-LIST] what must not change

Writing rules

Short beats.
3 sentences or fewer. Overloaded beats drift.
Numbers over adjectives.
Counts, distances, seconds.
Behavior, not feelings.
"Smiles, eyes stay the same", not "feels happy".
Tag, don't re-describe.
"@hero" plus what changes: clothes, action.

Director notes that work

Same shot. Change one thing: he runs forward, natural smile, eyes stay the same.
Same shot. The camera starts wide and ends inside the shoe's threads.
Same shot, but exactly 3 riders and an empty background.

In short

  • Use the same order every time.
  • Say what each reference image is for.
  • Give notes as a director would. Claude makes the edit.

Fix

Smallest fix first: one-line change, then simplify the shot, then rewrite from the best frame.

In short

  • One-line change, everything else word for word.
  • Max 4 rounds per scene, then change the shot design.
  • Hook, payoff and pack shot first. If those fail, the film fails.
